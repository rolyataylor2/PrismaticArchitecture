# Parts

Parts are learned, bounded Agent–world relationships or motivated perspectives. They may organize around people, animals, relationships, places, projects, tasks, recurring behavior, social patterns, institutions, concepts, responsibilities, other Parts, internal states, or classes of situations. They may be persistent or temporary, and need not be highly emotional or action-oriented.

Activated Parts receive the exact shared immutable WorldState and interpret it, relate it to their missions, react, and may forecast, think, contribute context, ask a question, or propose behavior. Significant activation change may be supplied as context and invite, but does not require, prediction. A Part normally carries one unresolved prediction; a prediction may precede or exist independently of a behavioral bid. Recognition/context Parts need not bid or predict.

Parts are language-first perspective bundles rather than collections of numeric weights. Their conceptual state may include identity, mission, worldview, beliefs, subjective memories, origin, trigger patterns, strategies, persistence/stickiness, expression, cognitive sophistication, predictions and lived results, completion/sunset understanding, lifecycle, lineage, and version identity. These are not a permanent field schema. Task persistence is continued relevance of Parts carrying a concern, not a global Agent goal subsystem.

Part language normally preserves a first-person boundary: a Part speaks as “I” and may advocate for the complete Agent using “we.” Its memories and beliefs may be expressed as reality from its own positioned perspective while remaining distinct from objective WorldState. Keep specific relational knowledge with the relevant relational Part and transferable lessons on generalized Parts where development supports that distinction. Historical provenance is not itself an activation trigger; activation propagation uses bounded cues.
