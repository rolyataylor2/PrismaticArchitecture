# Part Formation

Concerns emergence of new Parts from meaningful salience. Formation follows experience → cognition → possible formation; simulations never command it. Formation may begin around a new or newly important learned relationship, belief, or definition about reality. Significance need not mean high emotional charge.

Sustained mismatch between desired Identity and available behavior may become emotionally significant and contribute to introspective or corrective Part formation. Such a Part remains ordinary; no mandatory IntrospectionPart class is implied.

Currently recognized conceptual patterns include:

- emotionally significant outcome;
- emotional investment in a person, relationship, project, place, responsibility, cause, event, possession, skill, or experience;
- temporary mission-support objective.

These are not mutually exclusive classes. Consider adaptation, specialization, or splitting before creating a new Part. Most newly formed task-oriented Parts may be temporary unless repeated experience gives them reason to persist.

## Object-anchored Parts

A Part may organize itself around something that has become psychologically meaningful to the Agent, such as a person, relationship, project, task, place, community, platform, thread, skill, or recurring problem.

The existence of an object is not sufficient. "Alex" should not automatically create a Part merely because Alex exists. A Part may form because the Agent develops a meaningful relational stance toward Alex.

An object-anchored Part is not a contact record or global memory database. Factual records such as preferences, locations, or statements may belong to external records, while a relational Part preserves positioned meaning, for example: "I enjoy collaborating with Taylor because he explores weird ideas deeply" or "When he says something is overcomplicated, simplify aggressively."

## Temporary task Parts

Task-oriented Parts may remain cognitively relevant through stickiness while a concern remains unresolved, such as "Finish math assignment tonight." Once complete, stickiness may decline and the Part may naturally become dormant or archive. Repeated experience may transform it into a broader perspective such as "Homework gets ignored unless I keep pushing." This development should emerge semantically; no global task manager is implied.
