# Relational Consolidation

Relational consolidation looks for repeated structure across Parts activated during lived experience. A conceptual process is to inspect activated Parts and meaningful changes; identify repeated referents and co-activation; verify independent, meaningful evidence; check whether existing Parts represent the pattern; distinguish person-specific, situation-specific, and generalizable material; extract the minimum shared meaning; propose update/form/split/merge operations; carefully rewire retrieval surfaces; validate retained information; and commit conservatively.

For example, repeated experiences involving Paul may activate Cautious, Social Interest, and Boundary Protection. A Paul relational Part may be useful if Paul has sufficient repeated psychological significance and would reduce confusion or improve future cognition. A repeated noun alone is not enough.

Person-specific material can move into Paul while generic Parts retain transferable lessons. Do not simply duplicate every Paul-related sentence while leaving identical specific content scattered in generalized Parts. Preserve provenance to experiences and prior Part versions. Information moves are semantic proposals, not mechanical extraction.
# Relational Consolidation

Repeated psychologically meaningful interaction with a referent may suggest that a persistent relational Part would improve future cognition. Consolidation considers whether the referent matters repeatedly, whether a relational representation adds value, and whether current Part structure is already sufficient. Mention frequency by itself is not enough; weak evidence favors no structural change.

When a relational Part forms, person- or referent-specific learning may move toward it while transferable lessons remain with generalized Parts. Do not duplicate all material mechanically. Preserve source experience provenance, but do not leave the source referent on a generalized Part's active retrieval surface solely because it contributed to learning. Provenance is not a trigger.

Relational Parts may represent people, groups, communities, culture, institutions, roles, projects, tasks, places, concepts, internal states, recurring behaviors, social dynamics, or other Parts. They may be neutral, mildly charged, or emotional, and need not be defensive or action-oriented. No fixed hierarchy is implied. Documentation scaffold only.
