# Part Association Network

Parts may form a network of learned relationships. Online activation retains the current shared immutable WorldState and independent Jev activation Score per candidate. Bounded secondary cues may cause additional Parts to be considered; they do not replace the primary Activator or change objective WorldState. Cues should be constrained semantic context, not unrestricted Part prose. Any propagation must eventually terminate through bounded depth/budgets, duplicate suppression, cycle handling, relevance decay, inhibition, or stabilization; exact choices remain open.

Keep these concepts distinct:

- **Association:** concepts or Parts are meaningfully related and may inform candidate retrieval.
- **Trigger:** a present circumstance can make a Part psychologically relevant.
- **Provenance:** an experience or earlier state contributed to the Part's development; provenance normally does not activate it.
- **Lineage:** developmental ancestry through split, merge, or succession.
- **Memory reference:** evidence available to the Part that owns it or through explicit mediation.
- **Stickiness:** persistence of candidate consideration across cognitive cycles while a concern remains unresolved; it does not imply activation, association, or authority.

Associations do not grant authority or direct private-memory access. A Part may emit a bounded cue that leads the Activator to consider another Part. Current trigger patterns define present relevance. See [Relational Parts](../theory/RELATIONAL_PARTS.md) and [Activation Propagation](ACTIVATION_PROPAGATION.md).

Repeated co-activation is evidence for offline consolidation to inspect, not an automatic merge rule. Likewise, a repeated referent may suggest a relational Part only when it is psychologically significant and existing structure is insufficient. Consolidation remains conservative. Emotional charge is distinct from activation Score, stickiness, prediction confidence, and bid authority. Higher-order patterns such as attachment, culture, and metacognition may initially emerge through these learned relationships rather than mandatory standalone engines.
