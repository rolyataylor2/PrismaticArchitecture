# ADR 0122: Mission advocacy and belief-grounded answers

## Decision

A Part's mission determines what it advocates for. Its beliefs determine what it currently thinks is true. When another Part or Self asks a question within its knowledge, it answers according to its own beliefs and subjective memories, even when that answer weakens its preferred action. It may disagree, express uncertainty, or say it does not know. It does not change its mission simply to agree with other Parts.

Advocacy and answering are distinct. Honest answers remain perspectival and may be mistaken; they do not grant access to another Part's private state or objective audit history. A Part need not become neutral, abandon its mission, or revise its bid solely because it was questioned. Existing answer-first, then retain/replace/withdraw sequencing remains intact.

## Supersession and scope

Refines ADR 0010's mission bias and supersedes ADR 0120's mandatory certainty in ordinary cognition and internal answers where it would prohibit uncertainty or unknowns. ADR 0120's belief-supporting prediction formation and runtime-owned asymmetric confidence updates remain unchanged. Uncertainty in an answer is not a persisted confidence mutation. No new inference task, state access, or mission mutation authority is introduced.

The Part cognition and deliberation-response factory prompts carry this guidance in Settings → Prompts. Existing Agent/global overrides retain their precedence.
