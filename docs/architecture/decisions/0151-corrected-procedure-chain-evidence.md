# ADR 0151: Corrected procedure chain evidence

Status: Accepted implementation target extending ADRs 0147/0149.
Date: 2026-10-09

A reusable procedure learned from an ended corrected execution represents the effective whole procedure: each stopped segment's independently settled selected prefix, followed by the final selected remainder and its whole-result verification. It must not silently become a reusable fragment that omits completed work. Reconstruction is read-only procedural evidence outside cognition; it does not dispatch operations, select intentions or create psychological meaning.

Traverse immutable parent contracts and execution records through exact claim/ready receipts, with cycle, thread, selected bid, prefix and constraint continuity checked at every link. Preserve chronological segment identifiers, selected bids and checkpoint references. Reject cycles, missing records, unaccounted effects and mismatched relationships. Only a separately verified final segment can establish passed chain evidence. Historical evidence does not require today's tool grants or renew execution authority. Current grants and preferences remain mandatory when applying a learned method.

Use the original contract's whole-task limits for a fresh reusable procedure, never the final remainder's residual limits. Preserve final accumulated constraints and verification, and strip one-time bindings. If the reconstructed procedure exceeds the supported representation, retain an explicit inconclusive record rather than truncate its steps.

Consolidation must include all contributing cycles' expressed thoughts, selected and rejected bids, activation contexts and outcomes. Inference proposes conditional applicability and explains changes; runtime retains the evidenced operations and source relationships. A corrected skill remains a candidate until the applicable validation and approval policy permits use. Temporal success is not causal proof, and a thought alone does not change the method.

Inspection must show the initial instruction, each completed prefix, selected change, discarded future instruction and final result, alongside source products and any originating skill/version. Full-chain candidate construction, validation, inspection and normal-model repeated correction/transfer experiments are required implementation work; this decision does not declare them complete.
