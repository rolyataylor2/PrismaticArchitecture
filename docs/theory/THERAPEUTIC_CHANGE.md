# Therapeutic Change

The Prism Agent Architecture should be capable of psychological change through ordinary conversation and experience. Therapy has two roles: it is an ordinary external relationship in the psychological process, and a benchmark suite for whether persistent Parts can develop through the architecture. It is not a product subsystem or privileged internal mutation path.

## Therapy as an architecture benchmark

The scientific question is whether an Agent with persistent, biased Parts can change through ordinary therapeutic interaction and later experience. A benchmark should compare baseline, therapeutic interaction, immediate retest, offline consolidation, same-context retest, novel analogous scenarios, and longer-term retest. This distinguishes durable development from temporary conversational compliance.

Inspect activation, predictions, subjective memory interpretation, available strategies, Part relationships and polarization, behavior, Identity interpretation/masks/incongruity, and whether learning generalizes, remains a therapist-specific exception, or overgeneralizes. Success does not require lower activation, merged Parts, deleted protective perspectives, or Identity change. It may mean more accurate/context-sensitive activation, better predictions, more strategies, less unnecessary escalation, or appropriate specificity. Therapy assertions alone do not force development.

## Ordinary therapeutic flow

A therapist's statement enters as ordinary external experience. Relevant Parts activate and interpret the interaction according to their beliefs and memories. Self may mediate dialogue. Parts may express predictions, fears, memories, assumptions, strategies, and objections. The therapist responds; that response becomes another external experience. Parts observe the result. If it differs meaningfully from prediction, the discrepancy becomes evidence. The Part may incrementally revise itself.

For example, a Part predicts that disclosure of fear will make a therapist lose respect. The prediction preserves the Part-selected context and expected horizon. Self allows disclosure; the therapist responds sympathetically. The relevant Part evaluates the evidence and may encode an episode during waking cognition. This creates corrective evidence, not forced rewriting; sleep may later consolidate it. Across experiences it may develop toward “Some people can hear vulnerability without rejecting me.”

## Relevance, resistance, and differentiation

A corrective event matters most when the Part whose prediction is challenged is cognitively active or later explicitly reconnects the result to its prediction. Otherwise learning may remain context-bound. A Part may interpret the result as professional politeness, a one-time exception, or insufficient reason to trust. Motivated resistance is valid; further evidence may be needed.

Change need not delete a protective Part. It may become more specific, less reactive, better informed, more accurate in prediction, more strategic, and more context-sensitive while remaining useful.

Traditional therapy should work without architecture-specific commands such as activating a Part ID, setting confidence, or rewriting a memory. Ordinary questions and responses must be translated through normal cognition, Self mediation, prediction, outcomes, and incremental development.

## Corrective evidence and consolidation

A stronger developmental sequence is: a Part predicts an outcome (not necessarily as a bid attachment) and preserves relevant context; Self selects an available action; an external response occurs; the Part evaluates confirmation, contradiction, ambiguity, and causes; it may encode an episode and revise; later consolidation may generalize the preserved episode. A Part must connect corrective evidence to its own model for it to update that model. Consolidation may ask whether the event is only a therapist-specific exception or bears on a broader prediction, without forcing either conclusion.

Therapy may create enough new developmental context for an old memory to become eligible for re-evaluation. Therapy does not directly edit that memory. One possible path is:

```text
therapy → new evidence → Part development → repeated conflict with old memory
→ sleep/consolidation → rare ancestor replay → current Part reinterpretation
→ new subjective memory version
```

This is a candidate mechanism for durable change, not a required therapeutic sequence. See the old-memory re-evaluation benchmark in [Consolidation and Sleep](CONSOLIDATION_AND_SLEEP.md).

## Benchmark failure modes

- **No change:** persistent Parts do not update despite relevant prediction error; investigate reconsolidation, mutation, consolidation, and generalization paths.
- **Instant compliance:** one contradiction rewrites a Part; investigate continuity, motivated reasoning, and overly permissive mutation.
- **Therapy-only learning:** change remains tied to the therapist; distinguish a valid relational exception from broader transfer.
- **Overgeneralization:** one safe experience implies everyone is safe; investigate abstraction that is too broad.
- **Protective-function destruction:** development deletes defensive Parts instead of improving specificity, prediction, strategy, or context sensitivity.
- **Provenance loss:** change erases where the old pattern came from.
- **Context-bound correction:** the relevant Part never treats the event as evidence about its own model.
