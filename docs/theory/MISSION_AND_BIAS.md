# Mission and Bias

A Part exists in relation to a mission: what it tries to accomplish, protect, preserve, obtain, understand, prevent, or monitor. It evaluates reality partly by asking how circumstances affect that mission.

Bias is useful specialization, not automatically cognitive failure. Identical evidence may produce different interpretations because Parts attend to different social signals, dangers, opportunities, or subjective memories. A Part uses what it remembers as evidence supporting its mission-biased view of reality.

A mission may persist after circumstances change and produce maladaptation. Development can revise assumptions, strategies, trigger sensitivity, urgency, forecasting, and mission interpretation. A Part may enter dormancy or archival when its mission is complete.

Mission does not specify one behavior. “Keep us from being abandoned” might produce agreeableness, withdrawal, monitoring, confrontation, reassurance-seeking, or avoidance. Strategy is distinct from mission: a Part can preserve its underlying concern while replacing an ineffective strategy, and its role may evolve while remaining historically continuous.

## Ego-centric service and relational meaning

A Part's mission expresses what it believes best serves the complete Agent from its own perspective. “What best serves me is to pay attention to other people's openness” expresses the mission; “Test other people's responses” is a strategy; “Others may not want to be open to me” is a driving belief. Keep purpose, method, and assumption distinct.

Ego-centric service includes felt safety, connection, dignity, competence, care, curiosity, and ambition. It does not require selfish conduct or a defensive motive for every Part. A Part can be mistaken about what serves the Agent; conflicting meanings remain intentional. The Part supplies motivation and interpretation, while Self retains selection authority guided by Identity.

Names describe the Part's feeling or motivation in fewer than six words: “Need To Complete Homework” rather than “Do Homework”; “My Fear Of Dogs” rather than “Avoid Dog.” Names are descriptions, not identities or operation labels. Renaming preserves developmental continuity.

## Advocacy and answers

A mission determines what a Part advocates for; beliefs determine what it currently thinks is true. Answers to another Part or Self follow the owning Part's beliefs and memories, even when they weaken its preferred action. Disagreement, uncertainty and unknowns are permitted without changing the mission to agree. This refines ordinary cognition certainty under ADR 0122; belief-supporting prediction formation and runtime confidence rules remain unchanged.
