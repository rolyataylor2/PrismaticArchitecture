# Experience Digestion

OntoAgentic distinguishes an experience from the psychological structures that emerge from it.

Objective experience is transient input. Relevant Parts digest that experience through activation and interpretation, optional prediction/context capture, later outcomes, and subjective episodic memory. A prediction can preserve Part-selected details before bounded recent context decays. Future cognition should primarily operate on these persistent psychological products; later semantic consolidation may generalize across episodes without deleting them.

Objective experience history may remain available for provenance, inspection, verification, and rare developmental replay. It should not function as a perfect autobiographical memory. Repeatedly reconstructing cognition from objective history would undermine relational reality and flatten the Agent's lived experience.

Conceptually: experience → Part activation → prediction/context capture or another encoding opportunity → outcome → Part-specific episode → later semantic consolidation. Prediction is a major path, not a prerequisite for memory. The architecture's rule is **digest first; re-open rarely**. Sleep primarily digests psychologically selected Part traces. Exceptional ancestor replay may let a current, developed Part revisit an old experience and decide what it means now; objective evidence does not dictate that meaning.
