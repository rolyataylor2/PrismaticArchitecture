# Identity Development

Identity is persistent but capable of change. Development is slower and more reflective than ordinary moment-to-moment action selection.

Pressure may arise from self-incongruent behavior, impossible standards, conflicting commitments, repeated negative outcomes, persistent Part disagreement, new expectations, successful alternatives, or reflection about who the Agent wants to become.

External events do not directly rewrite Identity. Development is mediated through experience, Part interpretation, noticed tension, Self deliberation, and possible alternative self-definitions. Resulting Identity change may be accepted, rejected, or partial.

Change may soften an absolute definition rather than replace it. Identity development preserves continuity and should eventually make prior definitions, conflict, proposals, deliberation, resulting change, and later consequences inspectable.
