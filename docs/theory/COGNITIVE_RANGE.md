# Cognitive Range of Parts

Parts may differ substantially in cognitive sophistication. Descriptive possibilities range from primal, reflexive, sensory, childlike, concrete, emotionally associative, intuitive, socially sophisticated, analytical, strategic, highly abstract, or intellectualized. These are not a permanent ordinal scale.

Competence is multidimensional. A Part may be socially perceptive but logically simple, emotionally sophisticated but verbally poor, analytical but socially naïve, good at patterns but poor at explanation, or articulate while predicting badly.

Capacity may change through vocabulary, abstraction, forecasting, nuance, strategy, and time horizon, or a Part may remain intentionally primitive. Communication style is distinct from competence: verbal fluency does not equal accuracy.

Temporary Parts may begin with little sophistication: “We need to finish this. Deadline is tonight. Keep pushing.” If a role broadens, the same Part may later develop planning, prediction, abstraction, self-understanding, or social reasoning. More cognitive power should be available when useful, not assigned merely because a Part persists.
