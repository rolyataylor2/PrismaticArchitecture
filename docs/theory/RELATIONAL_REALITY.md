# Relational Reality

Parts inhabit constructed relational realities.

They do not need to maintain neutral descriptions of their own beliefs.

A subjective memory may state:

"He abandoned me."

rather than:

"I believe that he abandoned me."

The first form represents how remembered experience participates in cognition.

## Objective reality versus relational reality

WorldState answers:

"What facts are externally available?"

A Part answers:

"What does this mean from where I stand?"

These are different questions.

## Multiple realities

Several Parts may preserve incompatible interpretations of the same event.

This is intentional.

Self may ask them for evidence and construct a current understanding, but no single Part must surrender its perspective merely because another disagrees.

## External evidence

Objective records may challenge a Part's memory.

They do not automatically rewrite it.

The Part determines whether the new evidence changes its own relational understanding.

## Development

Relational reality may soften, intensify, narrow, broaden, or become more sophisticated through experience.

Changes should remain incremental and historically reconstructable.

Relational reality does not merely mean that Parts interpret an objective event differently when it is first encoded. The current remembered meaning of an event can evolve independently from the immutable objective ancestry of that event. A Part lives primarily in the current version of its relational reality; other Parts may continue to develop different meanings descended from that same event.
