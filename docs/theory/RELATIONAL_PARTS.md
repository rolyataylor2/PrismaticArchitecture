# Relational Parts

A Part can represent a learned relationship rather than only a defensive or goal-oriented strategy. Relational Parts may recognize a person, recurring context, role, project, concept, or internal experience and provide context from the Agent's history. They need not automatically produce a behavioral bid.

Emotional charge may vary independently from specificity. A specific low-reactivity relational Part should often be allowed to contextualize a situation before broad reactive Parts dominate it, where practical. Urgent direct triggers can interrupt this ordering.

Relational knowledge is subjective, not an objective contact record. A Paul Part may represent the Agent's learned relationship with Paul and make relevant context available without proposing action. Its private memory remains its own.
