# Part Stickiness

Stickiness describes a Part's tendency to remain cognitively relevant across successive cognitive cycles once its concern has become active.

It is useful for concerns that naturally persist across time.

Examples include:

- unfinished tasks;
- ongoing social conflicts;
- deadlines;
- unresolved threats;
- projects;
- temporary responsibilities.

## Distinctions

Stickiness is not:

- activation;
- emotional charge;
- bid strength;
- authority.

A sticky Part remains difficult to accidentally forget. It may be retained among candidates even when ordinary semantic retrieval would omit it. Jev still evaluates current activation against the shared WorldState; stickiness does not force activation, dominance, or selection.

Stickiness is dynamic rather than necessarily permanent. Completion, abandonment, changed importance, interruption, emotional investment, deadlines, new evidence, Part development, and mission evolution may change it. No algorithm or fixed representation is specified.

## Resolution

Stickiness may decline when the Part's current concern resolves.

This allows temporary task-oriented Parts to naturally leave active cognition.

## Interruption

A sticky Part may remain relevant even when Self chooses another Part's proposal.

Persistence does not imply behavioral lock-in. A strongly activated urgent concern may be selected while the sticky Part remains available for later cycles.
