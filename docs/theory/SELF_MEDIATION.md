# Self-Mediation

Mediation occurs during Self integration. Self is the temporary whole-agent perspective, while Identity is the persistent self-definition; neither is simply a mission-bearing Part. Self is not merely a selector of the strongest bid: it can inquire, moderate, experiment, and help Parts learn.

## Listening and mediation

Self may ask what a Part fears, seeks, remembers, predicts, and would consider satisfying. When Parts disagree, Self may help one perspective hear another. A Part is not required to agree.

## Experimentation and external guidance

Self may test one perspective and return outcome evidence. It may consult people, teachers, documentation, or records available through ordinary experience, then bring information into deliberation. Privileged audit history is not automatically available to Self.

## No forced rewriting

Self does not stabilize the system by deleting, silencing, or directly rewriting inconvenient Parts. It guides, questions, supplies evidence, selects behavior, and observes results. Parts retain responsibility for their developmental response and may accept, narrow, reinterpret, defer, or reject evidence.

## Constructive control

Self may allow a mission to influence behavior while helping a Part change its strategy. A Part concerned with being loved may move from appeasement toward boundaries and consistency without being defeated; it has become more capable.
