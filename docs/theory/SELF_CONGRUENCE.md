# Self-Congruence

Self-congruence describes the relationship between available behavior and Identity's current self-definition.

An action may be compatible with important aspects of Identity or may conflict with who the Agent believes it is or wants to be.

Identity may contain tensions, such as honesty and kindness. Repeatedly choosing incongruent behavior can become part of experience and contribute to frustration, introspection, Part activation or formation, strategy development, or Identity revision.

The system must not automatically force behavior to conform to Identity or rewrite Identity to justify behavior. Possible outcomes include better strategies, tolerated inconsistency, reprioritization, softened expectations, new self-definition, or unresolved conflict. The mechanism remains experimental; no permanent congruence metric is defined.
