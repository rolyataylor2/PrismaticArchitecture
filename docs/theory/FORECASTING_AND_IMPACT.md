# Forecasting and Impact

Parts predict how situations may affect their missions. A Part may consider expected, best plausible, and worst plausible outcomes, with different Parts assigning radically different probability or importance.

Consequences occur across timescales: immediate, near-term, delayed, long-term, or uncertain. A reactive Part may overweight immediacy; another may reason across years. This difference should not automatically be normalized away.

A Part evaluates predicted outcomes relative to its mission, including positive or negative effect, reversibility, duration, and confidence. No permanent numeric formula is defined.

Afterward, it may compare expectation with reality, timing, severity, strategy effectiveness, and missed evidence. Repeated errors may contribute to changed confidence, triggers, strategies, reasoning, specialization, merging, or dormancy; repeated success may also cause development.
